# Introduction
The main change that occurred between 7500 BCE and 6000 BCE in Southwest Asia (Near East) was production of pottery. While ceramic figurines used to be produced since Late Paleolithic period [^ceramic_figurines], and pottery started to be produced in East Asia as early as around 18 000 BCE, in Fertile Crescent this invention followed start of cultivation of cereals and domestication of animals. This facilitated ongoing shift to sedentary life, allowing for safer storage of wild cereals and help in cooking due to superior thermal retention and even heat distribution. Due to its inherent durability, pottery now serves as a primary source of archaeological data, often surviving as diagnostic fragments long after artifacts made of organic materials have decayed [^pottery_in_archeology].
Pre-Pottery Neolithic A/B cultures are replaced, towards 6000 BCE by regional cultures, like Halaf or Ubaid, differing in style of produced pottery and other artifacts. North African deserts experience spread of pastoralism where nomadic lifestyle involves domestication of animals. One of the most important sites, modern Çatalhöyük, rises to prominence in preceding millennium. It is especially notable for its large size, apparent egalitarian social structure, and value as a well-preserved example of early Neolithic-era permanent human settlements.[^chatalhoyuk]
