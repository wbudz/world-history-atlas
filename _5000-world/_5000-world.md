# Introduction
Around 5000 BCE, communities across the world followed diverse ways of life. Farming and settled communities were established in some regions, while many others relied on hunting, gathering, and fishing, or combined these with cultivation and herding. Even in regions where the Neolithic period emerged, farming, herding, pottery, and sedentism took place at different times and in different combinations. 
Fertile Crescent area of Southwest Asia was arguably the strongest concentration of sedentary societies but farming and herding were already spreading to new lands. Agriculture and domestication of animals reached large parts of Europe until 5000 BCE, though gradually and unevenly, appearing first in southeastern and central parts of the continent. Neolithic advancements were also independently spreading to Egypt around Faiyum and areas west of Indus Valley in the form of earliest Mehrgarh culture. In some areas like Northern Sahara this gave rise to pastoral nomadism where animal domestication was adopted by mobile communities. This happened some time before the end of African Humid Period, a gradual shift that transformed savannas and grasslands suitable for sustained human use to arid semideserts and deserts[^african_humid_period].
Neolithic cultures are also developing independently in other parts of the world. In China, millet cultivation was developing in the north and rice cultivation in the Yangtze region. In parts of the Americas, plant domestication and cultivation were already underway, but farming remained localized and did not yet define life across either region[^centres_of_origin].
