# Introduction
The final division of the Stone Age saw Neolithic Revolution, a wide-ranging set of developments that brought introduction of farming, domestication of animals, and change from a hunter-gatherer lifestyle to one of settlement. It happened at different places of world independent of each other and spread in following millennia [^spread_of_agriculture].
The map concentrates on development of Neolithic in the oldest of those centres, called Fertile Crescent. It formed around lands of Natufian culture in Levant which already pioneered use of wild cereals. It is possible that people already dependent on wild cereals in their diet and more sedentary way of life developed sustained farming after 10 000 BCE[^neolithic].
Early Neolithic age farming was limited to a narrow range of plants, both wild and domesticated, which included einkorn wheat and figs, and the keeping of dogs. By about 8000 BCE, it included domesticated sheep and goats, cattle and pigs. Not all of these cultural elements characteristic of the Neolithic appeared everywhere in the same order: the earliest farming societies in Near East did not use pottery until about 7000-6700 BCE[^near_east_pottery].
