# Introduction
Besides the Neolithic Revolution in the Fertile Crescent (the area around Mesopotamia in the Near East) around 9000 BCE, another independent centre of agricultural development emerged in East Asia at roughly the same time.[^centres_of_origin] It developed across two major river basins — the Yellow River and the Yangtze — each with different ecological conditions, and over time their farmers exchanged crops and practices. The map highlights the key Neolithic cultures of the early period.

Although the shift toward farming in East Asia began at roughly the same time as in the Near East, it followed a different trajectory. East Asian farmers cultivated rice and millet rather than the Near Eastern cereals (einkorn and emmer wheat, barley) and legumes (lentil, pea, bitter vetch, chickpea). Animal herding was a major feature of the Neolithic transition in the Near East, whereas domestication in China was comparatively limited and centred mainly on pigs. By contrast, pottery developed much earlier in East Asia and predates farming there by a wide margin (around 18,000 BCE, compared with roughly 7000-6700 BCE in the Fertile Crescent).

# Notes
